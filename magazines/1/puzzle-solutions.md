## Puzzle 2 — Match the place settings

The reader matches each guest to a place setting. More than one setting shares a detail, including lemon slices, so the finished puzzle will need combined deductions.

| Guest     | Seat         | Other         | Glass        | Napkin  |
| --------- | ------------ | ------------- | ------------ | ------- |
| Alexander | Head (top)   | Nothing       | Orange slice | Regular |
| Alice     | Top left     | Nothing       | Lemon slice  | Regular |
| Robert    | Bottom right | No soup spoon | Lemon slice  | Regular |
| Carl      | Top right    | No bread      | Orange slice | Folded  |
| Daniel    | Bottom left  | Nothing       | Nothing      | Folded  |

This is a provisional answer table. The settings can be changed; the important design constraint is that individual attributes overlap, so no guest is identified by a single giveaway detail. We give away Alexander's position at the head, at some point we mention Alice being his left hand. We can say Carl is Celiac for the clue of not having bread (it could work with no butter knife because he is lactose intolerant or similar approach that can be easily drawn in the picture and clear to the reader).

## Puzzle 4 — The host's private encounters

The reader cross-references details in the guests' accounts with household records to place each private encounter between two timed events. The clues should overlap, so no single account or record gives away an encounter's position by itself. The answer is the order of encounters, not an exact time for every conversation.

| Order | Guest  | Encounter falls between                                                            |
| ----- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Alice  | The host's second cigarette (7:15) and the east hall lamps coming on (7:30)        |
| 2     | Robert | The east hall lamps coming on (7:30) and the tea trolley leaving the pantry (7:45) |
| 3     | Carl   | The tea trolley passing the drawing room (7:50) and the dinner gong (8:00)         |
| 4     | Daniel | The dinner gong (8:00) and the slow hall clock striking eight (actual time 8:10)   |

This is a provisional timeline structure. The account details and household records should be presented separately, so readers have to cross-reference them rather than copy times from the interviews.

| Source                | Detail to include                                                                 |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Alice's account       | The host had just put away his cigarette case; the east hall was still dark.      |
| Robert's account      | The east hall lamps were already on; the tea trolley had not yet left the pantry. |
| Carl's account        | The tea trolley had passed the drawing room; the dinner gong had not yet rung.    |
| Daniel's account      | The dinner gong had rung; the hall clock then struck eight.                       |
| Butler's smoking note | The host put away his second cigarette at 7:15.                                   |
| Lighting record       | The east hall lamps came on at 7:30.                                              |
| Pantry slate          | The tea trolley left the pantry at 7:45 and passed the drawing room at 7:50.      |
| Kitchen note          | The dinner gong rang at 8:00.                                                     |
| Clock repair card     | The hall clock is ten minutes slow, so it struck eight at 8:10.                   |

Cross-referencing those details places each encounter within a different interval. This keeps the task focused on building a timeline from testimony and records, rather than directly matching four named guests to four exact times.
