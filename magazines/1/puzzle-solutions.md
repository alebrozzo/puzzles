## Puzzle 2 — Match the place settings

The reader matches each guest to a place setting. More than one setting shares a detail, including lemon slices, so the finished puzzle will need combined deductions.

| Guest | Seat  | Other         | Glass        | Napkin  |
| ----- | ----- | ------------- | ------------ | ------- |
| Alice | North | Nothing       | Lemon slice  | Regular |
| Bob   | East  | No soup spoon | Lemon slice  | Regular |
| Carl  | South | No bread      | Orange slice | Folded  |
| Dave  | West  | Nothing       | Nothing      | Regular |

This is a provisional answer table. The settings can be changed later; the important design constraint is that individual attributes overlap, so no guest is identified by a single giveaway detail.

## Puzzle 3 — Motives and the detective's findings

| Guest | Possible motive                                                           | Evidence found by the detective                                | Where the evidence was found |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Alice | Prevent a decades-old accident from being investigated again              | A newspaper clipping with a handwritten correction on the back | Host's study                 |
| Bob   | Keep a private debt from becoming known to his family                     | A pawn ticket tucked inside an old gardening book              | Conservatory                 |
| Carl  | Prevent the host from revealing that they once supplied a false alibi     | A telegram naming the date of the old case                     | Music room                   |
| Dave  | Stop the host from revealing a concealed change of identity in his family | A baptism record with an altered name                          | Coatroom desk                |

These motives are not intended to follow obviously from each guest's occupation or relationship to the host. They are leads, not proof of guilt. The four secrets may need a shared backstory explaining why the host knew about all of them and why the guests were invited to this dinner; that common thread is still undecided.
