## Puzzle 4 — Detective's findings

Solution:

| Guest  | Possible motive                                | Evidence found by the detective                            | Where the evidence was found      |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Alice  | Vale was cutting her out of their business     | A letter to the board of directors                         | Desk drawer                       |
| Robert | Sold Vale a forged item                        | An certificate of a painting claiming it's not an original | Inside a folder in a file cabinet |
| Carl   | Vale would retract a false alibi               | A newspaper clipping with a handwritten text on the back   | Pants front pocket                |
| Daniel | Needed funds that Vale would leave in his will | Vale's will                                                | Inside the safe                   |

Later on we will say that the newspaper clipping was on his pocket because he showed it to Carl saying he'd be retracting his statement

---

Guests: Alice, Robert, Carl, Daniel
Possible motives: losing business, Retract alibi, Needed money, Forged piece of art
Evidence: Letter, Will, Newspaper clipping, Certificate
Where was the evidence found: Safe, Desk, Cabinet, Pants pocket

---

Narration:
----------

“I’m done examining the murder scene,” the detective announced. “And I’m surprised by just how _much_ I’ve found. Usually, by the time someone has gone through the victim’s belongings, we have trouble finding clues. Yet here, I’ve found more than in any of my previous cases.”

“I imagine you found all of it in Alexander’s safe?” Robert asked.

“Strangely enough, no,” the detective replied. “His will was the only thing of interest in there. Well, that and some cash, but that’s hardly evidence. Of tax evasion, perhaps, but nothing useful to this case. I did, however, find something in the cabinet that concerns you specifically.”

“What could you possibly find that concerns me? I loved the old man!” exclaimed the art dealer.

“Well, I doubt most of us were mentioned in his will, so that tells you who the killer is, doesn’t it?” Alice interrupted, fixing her gaze on Carl.

“What?!” Carl squeaked. “I had NO reason to kill him! I’m not even in his will! You’re the one who was about to be kicked out of your own company, but now you’ll probably get to lead it instead!”

“I was going to be WHAT?!” Alice shouted, even louder than Carl. “Is that what the will says?” she demanded, turning to the detective.

“Not the will, no,” he replied. “But it’s true.”

“And Daniel has some very good motives of his own,” Carl continued, having regained his composure. A smile even crept across his face. “Don’t you, Doctor?”

“You absolute piece of... something!” the doctor snapped. “As if you’re such a model citizen! What about that accident you caused, the one your uncle covered up for you? You’d be in jail by now if he hadn’t provided you with a false alibi! Everyone here knows it!”

“I didn’t know...” the art dealer murmured, rather foolishly.

Everyone immediately turned to look at him. For a few seconds, nobody said a word, as though they were waiting for him to air some more dirty laundry. But he had nothing else to add.

“Say, didn’t you sell my uncle a fake painting? He told me all about it. We talked quite a lot, he and I. You see, money wasn’t what bound us together, so we could have a genuine relationship. Detective Thorne, I suppose you found a certificate of... of... whatever you call it when a painting is fake, among the papers in his desk?”

“All I found there was an unrelated but rather interesting letter,” the detective replied. “However, I also found a newspaper clipping elsewhere that concerns you. So, for the time being, everyone here remains a suspect.”
