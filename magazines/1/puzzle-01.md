Narration:
----------

"I've gathered you all here in the library because I need to know who you are and where you were at the time of the murder. Besides, it seemed to be the room with the most comfortable chairs."

"But why aren't the staff here as well? It looks as if you've already decided who the murderer is!"

"The cook, the butler and the maid were together the whole time, clearing up and preparing the coffee and sweets you were all meant to have after dinner."

"Speaking of which, I could do with a cannoli or two," said the oldest of the men.

"Ladies first," said Detective Thorne, ignoring the remark. "Mrs...?"

"Harcourt. Alice Harcourt. But Alice is fine."

"All right, Mrs. Harcourt..."

"Alice."

"Alice. Would you mind telling me where you were when the body was found?"

"Well, that depends on when it was found. But when I heard about Alexander's accident... or... whatever, I was skimming through his copy of The Odyssey."

"Of course you were," cut in a well-dressed man in his twenties. "Showing everyone how clever you are with your fancy books. God forbid anyone should catch you reading a comic!"

"As if you'd know what The Odyssey is," Alice shot back. "The longest thing you've ever read is a menu. And don't get me started on art. You'd look at a Picasso and ask who broke it."

The doctor watched them with a smirk, then turned to the detective. "Carl and Alice don't get along very well."

Thorne said nothing. He loved it when suspects talked without being asked. In his experience, it was the best way to pick up unexpected clues, and sometimes the guilty party would tell one lie too many and crack the case wide open.

"Anyway," the doctor went on, "I was looking for my coat when I noticed the house getting... louder. This place is far too big and badly heated, especially for an old man like me."

"Speaking of Picasso," said a man who hadn't spoken yet, turning to the doctor, "did you notice the one I recently found for Alexander?" Thorne could tell he was itching to show everyone how cultured he was.

"No, I haven't been in the gallery today," the doctor replied.

"Oh, the Picasso isn't in the gallery," the dealer added quickly. "Alexander said a painting with such an exquisite guitar belonged in the conservatory. Personally, I think the Carrington he bought while I was in London would have been better off in there too. I was examining it when I heard the terrible news, and let me tell you, it does not deserve the central spot he gave it in the gallery."

"I'm still chilly. And hungry. Carl, could I borrow your coat?"

"There's no way it would fit you, Daniel. It's tailor-made. And, well... you could stand to follow your own advice and get a bit more exercise, couldn't you?"

"I'll do that when you finish The Odyssey. I see Alice has already put it back," he added, pointing at a well-lit shelf. "Why don't you start now? You'll be done by the time you're fifty."

"At least I don't spend the whole evening shut away, puffing like a chimney. Maybe you'd have found your coat if you could see through all that cigar smoke you left in there. Honestly, you're a farce of a doctor. That clinic of yours would have gone bankrupt years ago if it weren't for my uncle's money."

An awkward silence followed. The detective simply waited to see who would be uncomfortable enough to break it. In the end, it was the art dealer.

"Anyway, I believe you wanted to begin your enquiries with Alexander's business partner..."

"Oh, there's no need," said the detective with a smile. "I already have everything I need."
