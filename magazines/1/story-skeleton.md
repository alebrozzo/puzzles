[text](story-skeleton.md)# The Last Dinner at Vale House — story skeleton

This draft defines the case structure and the facts each puzzle reveals. It does not create final clue wording or artwork. Names are treated as final but could change if it adds to the story.

## Case premise and solution

Alexander Vale hosts dinner at Vale House. After the meal, he goes to his study to fetch an old auction catalogue for Robert, the art dealer. While coffee is prepared, the butler enters the study and finds Vale dead.

Carl, Vale’s nephew, is the murderer. Years earlier, Carl caused a hit-and-run collision. Vale gave him a false alibi and helped the incident disappear. Vale has decided to correct that lie and give a statement to the police. During a private conversation before dinner, Vale tells Carl that he will no longer protect him.

Carl uses the narrow service passage that joins the drawing room, gallery, and study. He takes a small bronze horse statuette from the gallery, strikes Vale with it, and hides it in the torn lining of his coat.

## Final-puzzle design

The final puzzle is a **case-file grid**. Readers write the four names against their motive, route to the study, and whether that route could be used unseen. Alongside the grid is a short weapon-evidence box: it asks which moved object matches the wound, where the other objects ended up, and who could have collected the matching object without being seen.

Every suspect has a motive. Alice and Daniel have routes to the study, but the coffee service means either would have been seen using them. Robert cannot leave the conservatory. Carl alone has an unseen route through the service passage, which passes the gallery shelf where the bronze horse stood.

| Guest  | Motive learned in interview                | Route / opportunity                                        | Object fact                                 |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Alice  | Vale was cutting her out of their business | Library to main hall; visible to the butler serving coffee | Brass candlestick by the library telephone  |
| Robert | He sold Vale a forged painting             | Conservatory; no usable route to the study                 | Marble bust in Vale’s bedroom               |
| Carl   | Vale would retract the false alibi         | Drawing room to service passage; unseen                    | Bronze horse missing from the gallery shelf |
| Daniel | Vale could expose diverted clinic funds    | Gallery to study; visible to the maid carrying coffee      | Iron bookend on a gallery side table        |

The reader can therefore identify Carl only after bringing together facts from earlier, varied puzzles. The detective finds the bronze horse in Carl’s torn coat lining after the reader makes the accusation.

Robert had no route; Alice and Daniel had visible routes. After the reader identifies Carl, the detective searches his coat and finds the bronze horse in the torn lining.            |

## Why the interviews now work

Each interview is a different kind of puzzle and has a self-contained answer:

| Interview | Puzzle type                | Immediate answer                                   | Later value                                               |
| --------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Alice     | Company-ownership table    | Vale intended to remove Alice from the business.   | Motive; candlestick location; route is visible.           |
| Robert    | Forgery check              | The painting is a forgery.                         | Motive; marble-bust location; no route from conservatory. |
| Daniel    | Clinic money trail         | Vale discovered that Daniel diverted clinic funds. | Motive; bookend location; gallery route is visible.       |
| Carl      | Rebuild the newspaper page | Vale gave Carl a false alibi.                      | Motive; access to the unseen service passage.             |

The reader can enjoy each one as a financial, visual, arithmetic, or word-reconstruction puzzle. Its importance only becomes fully clear when the final case-file grid puts all four together.

## Details still open

- Vale’s final name, the house name, and the book title.
- The final wording and severity of the old hit-and-run and the other three secrets.
- Whether Robert knowingly sold the forgery.
- The exact amount and circumstances of Daniel’s diverted clinic funds.
- Individual clue wording, difficulty ratings, illustrations, and page count.
