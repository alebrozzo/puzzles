# The Last Dinner at Vale House — revised story skeleton

This draft gives the case a complete solution but does not create the final puzzle clues or artwork. Names remain placeholders.

## Case premise

Walter Vale hosts an ordinary dinner at Vale House. After the meal, he leaves the dining room to fetch an old auction catalogue from his study. The guests disperse through the house while coffee is prepared. The butler enters the study to summon Vale and finds him dead.

The murderer is **Carl, Vale’s nephew**. Years earlier, Carl caused a hit-and-run collision. Vale gave him a false alibi and helped the incident disappear. Vale has now decided to correct that lie and give a statement to the police. During a private conversation before dinner, Vale tells Carl that he will no longer protect him. Carl kills Vale before he can act.

Carl uses the narrow service passage that joins the drawing room, gallery, and study. He takes a small bronze horse statuette from the gallery, strikes Vale with it, wraps it in his folded dinner napkin, and conceals it in the torn lining of his coat.

## The four guests and their reasons to fear Vale

The detective discovers these reasons by searching the study in Puzzle 3; Vale does not announce them to the dinner party.

| Guest | Relationship to Vale | What the detective finds |
|---|---|---|
| Alice | Business partner | A draft agreement showing that Vale intended to sell his controlling interest and remove Alice from the business she helped build. |
| Bob | Art dealer | An expert report showing that the painting Bob sold Vale is a forgery, together with Bob’s invoice for it. It remains undecided whether Bob was deceived himself or knowingly sold it as genuine. |
| Carl | Nephew | Vale’s signed false alibi from the old collision and a draft statement in which Vale admits that he lied for Carl. |
| Dave | Doctor | Documents that could expose a concealed change of identity in Dave’s family. The exact history can be decided later. |

All four have a motive. The story does not treat motive alone as proof of murder.

## Puzzle-by-puzzle story

| # | Puzzle and possible form | What the reader does | Exact discovery and story movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The body is discovered** — four-set logic grid | Match each guest to their relationship with Vale, their location when the butler finds the body, and what they say they were doing. | Introduces Alice (business partner), Bob (art dealer), Carl (nephew), and Dave (doctor). Their accounts are: Alice was using the library telephone; Bob was examining the disputed painting in the conservatory; Carl was writing in a notebook in the drawing room; Dave was looking for his coat in the gallery. These are claims, not yet confirmed alibis. |
| 2 | **The dinner table** — visual place-setting match | Match each guest to a seat using overlapping details: glass garnish, napkin fold, missing spoon or bread, and place position. | Establishes who sat where during dinner. Carl’s place has the only folded napkin. Vale leaves the table after dinner to retrieve the old auction catalogue from his study. This gives a natural reason for Vale to be alone in the study and plants the napkin that later connects Carl to the weapon. |
| 3 | **Reasons to fear Vale** — four-set logic grid | Match each guest to a motive, a document found by the detective, and the place where it was found. | The detective uncovers the four exact pressures on the guests: Alice’s planned removal from the business, Bob’s forged painting, Carl’s false alibi, and Dave’s family record. None of the guests needed to be invited for a secret purpose. These are private matters Vale kept in his study. |
| 4 | **Vale’s private meetings** — timeline puzzle | Order Vale’s four pre-dinner conversations by cross-referencing the guests’ accounts with household events, such as the arrival of the tea tray, the laying of the table, and the call to dinner. | Carl’s conversation occurs last. Vale tells him that he intends to correct the false alibi he gave years ago. Alice, Bob, and Dave each spoke privately with Vale earlier about ordinary business, the painting, and family matters. The timeline shows why Carl had an immediate reason to panic, while all four still remain plausible suspects. |
| 5 | **The study before and after** — spot-the-difference scene puzzle | Compare the house steward’s pre-dinner inventory drawing with the study and adjoining gallery after the murder. | Four blunt objects are no longer in their normal display places: a bronze horse statuette, a marble bust, a brass candlestick, and a heavy iron bookend. The west study door also stands open. At this stage, all four objects could have caused the wound, and all four might have been moved for innocent reasons. The puzzle creates a fair list of weapon candidates instead of naming one immediately. |
| 6 | **The hidden way through Vale House** — literal maze and floor-plan puzzle | Trace every usable route from the guests’ rooms to the study. Doors that are locked, blocked, or one-way are built into the maze. | The conservatory is a dead end during dinner: its inner door is locked from the gallery side and its garden door is bolted. Bob could not reach the study from where he was examining the painting. The drawing room, gallery, and study are linked by a narrow service passage that avoids the main hall. Carl could use it from the drawing room; Dave could use it from the gallery; Alice could still have reached the study by the main corridor. Bob is the first suspect cleared. |
| 7 | **Which missing object was used?** — shape-matching puzzle | Match the shape of the fatal wound and small fragments at the desk to the four moved blunt objects from Puzzle 5. | The raised hoof of the bronze horse matches the wound and the tiny metal fragments found beside the desk. The other objects receive ordinary explanations: the candlestick had been moved to the dining table for the meal; Bob had placed the marble bust in the conservatory beside the painting for comparison; and Alice took the bookend to the library to hold the copied business papers flat. The bronze horse is the murder weapon. |
| 8 | **Interview: Alice** — document comparison puzzle | Compare the original business agreement, Vale’s planned amendment, and Alice’s annotations. | Alice admits that Vale intended to remove her from the business by selling his controlling interest to a rival. She also admits entering the study earlier that evening to copy part of the amendment, and she explains why the bookend was in the library. This proves access and gives her a stronger motive, but it does not place her in the study after dinner. Alice remains a viable suspect. |
| 9 | **Interview: Bob** — painting-authentication puzzle | Compare the painting’s signature, catalogue entry, restoration marks, and invoice. | The painting is a forgery. The reader learns why Bob feared Vale, and that Bob may have known more than he first admitted. Bob confirms that he moved the marble bust to the conservatory before dinner to compare its maker’s mark with the painting’s frame. The maze has already shown that Bob could not reach the study later, so he remains important to the story but is not the murderer. |
| 10 | **Interview: Dave** — call-record and statement puzzle | Reconstruct Dave’s telephone consultation from the answering-service record, the patient’s message, and the gallery telephone log. | Dave was on a sustained medical call from the gallery from shortly after Vale left the table until the body was discovered. The call is supported by an outside caller and the answering service. Dave’s family secret remains real, but he could not have taken the bronze horse and killed Vale. Dave is the only additional suspect cleared through an interview. Alice and Carl remain. |
| 11 | **Interview: Carl** — object-trace comparison puzzle | Compare Carl’s notebook, his folded napkin, the study-door traces, and the bronze horse’s base. | Carl claims he stayed in the drawing room writing in his notebook. The notebook cover bears an impression from the base of the bronze horse and contains tiny bronze flakes. A torn fragment of Carl’s folded napkin is caught in the west study door; its herringbone weave matches the napkin from Carl’s place at dinner. Carl used the notebook and napkin while carrying the statue through the service passage, so his writing story is a cover. |
| 12 | **Final case file: who killed Vale?** — evidence-synthesis deduction | Use the recorded facts to assign the route, weapon, motive, and physical traces to one person. | Carl is the only person who fits every fact. Bob lacked a route to the study; Dave was on the confirmed call; Alice had motive and access but no evidence linking her to the bronze horse or service passage. Carl had the immediate reason to silence Vale, a direct route from the drawing room, the bronze-horse impression on his notebook, and the napkin fragment at the study door. After the reader identifies him, the detective searches Carl’s coat and finds the bronze horse in the torn lining, wrapped in the remainder of the folded napkin. |

## Case-file facts for the finale

The final puzzle can ask readers to record these facts as they work through the book:

1. Vale left dinner and went alone to his study.
2. Four blunt objects had been moved from their normal places; the bronze horse caused the fatal wound.
3. Bob could not leave the conservatory to reach the study.
4. Dave was on a confirmed medical call in the gallery.
5. Alice had a real motive and access, but no link to the bronze horse or service passage.
6. Vale’s old false alibi gave Carl the immediate reason to act.
7. Carl’s notebook, folded napkin, and coat lining connect him to the bronze horse and the service route.
