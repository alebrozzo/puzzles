[text](story-skeleton.md)# The Last Dinner at Vale House — story skeleton

This draft defines the case structure and the facts each puzzle reveals. It does not create final clue wording or artwork. Names remain placeholders.

## Case premise and solution

Walter Vale hosts dinner at Vale House. After the meal, he goes to his study to fetch an old auction catalogue for Bob, the art dealer. While coffee is prepared, the butler enters the study and finds Vale dead.

Carl, Vale’s nephew, is the murderer. Years earlier, Carl caused a hit-and-run collision. Vale gave him a false alibi and helped the incident disappear. Vale has decided to correct that lie and give a statement to the police. During a private conversation before dinner, Vale tells Carl that he will no longer protect him.

Carl uses the narrow service passage that joins the drawing room, gallery, and study. He takes a small bronze horse statuette from the gallery, strikes Vale with it, wraps it in his folded dinner napkin, and hides it in the torn lining of his coat.

## Final-puzzle design

The final puzzle is a **case-file grid**. Readers write the four names against their motive, route to the study, whether that route could be used unseen, and the relevant blunt object or object trace.

Every suspect has a motive. Alice and Dave have routes to the study, but the coffee service means either would have been seen using them. Bob cannot leave the conservatory. Carl alone has an unseen route through the service passage and a physical connection to the bronze horse.

| Guest | Motive learned in interview                   | Route / opportunity                                        | Object fact                                            |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Alice | Vale was cutting her out of their business    | Library to main hall; visible to the butler serving coffee | Brass candlestick by the library telephone             |
| Bob   | He sold Vale a forged painting                | Conservatory; no usable route to the study                 | Marble bust in Vale’s bedroom                          |
| Carl  | Vale would retract the false alibi            | Drawing room to service passage; unseen                    | Herringbone napkin trace links him to the bronze horse |
| Dave  | Vale held documents about his family identity | Gallery to study; visible to the maid carrying coffee      | Iron bookend in the gallery                            |

The reader can therefore identify Carl only after bringing together facts from earlier, varied puzzles. The detective finds the bronze horse in Carl’s torn coat lining after the reader makes the accusation.

Their accounts are: Alice was using the library telephone; Bob was examining the disputed painting in the conservatory; Carl was writing in a notebook in the drawing room; Dave was looking for his coat in the gallery. These are claims, not confirmed alibis.                                                                          |
| 2   | **The dinner table** — visual place-setting match                          | Match each guest to a seat using overlapping details: glass garnish, napkin fold, missing spoon or bread, and place position.                                      | Establishes the dinner arrangement. Carl’s place has the only folded napkin with a herringbone weave. Vale leaves the table after dinner to retrieve an auction catalogue from the study. At this point the reader does not know which guest each item concerns or why it matters. The puzzle establishes the material that the interviews will later explain.                                                                           |

The reader learns only that Carl left the study visibly upset; the reason is held back until Carl’s interview. This creates a small unease without identifying the murderer.                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 5   | **The study before and after** — spot-the-difference scene puzzle          | Compare the house steward’s pre-dinner inventory drawing with the study and adjoining gallery after the murder.                                                    | Four blunt objects are no longer in their normal display places: a bronze horse statuette, a marble bust, a brass candlestick, and a heavy iron bookend. The west study door also stands open. All four objects are plausible weapon candidates, and none is treated as the answer yet.                                                                                                                                             |
| 6   | **The hidden way through Vale House** — literal maze and floor-plan puzzle | Trace every usable route from the guests’ rooms to the study. Bob cannot reach the study from where he was examining the painting. The drawing room, gallery, and study are linked by a narrow service passage that avoids the main hall. Alice, Carl, and Dave still have possible routes; Bob is the first suspect cleared.                                                       |
| 7   | **Which missing object was used?** — shape-matching puzzle                 | Match the fatal wound and small fragments at the desk to the shapes and materials of the four moved objects from Puzzle 5.                                         | The raised hoof of the bronze horse matches the wound and the fragments beside the desk. The reader learns the weapon, but not who used it. The other three objects remain part of the case file because their locations later explain why they were absent from their usual places.                                                                                                                                                |
| 8   | **Interview: Alice** — business-ledger deduction                           | Reconstruct a set of company payments, ownership shares, and proposed transfers.                                                                                   | The draft agreement from Puzzle 3 concerns Alice: Vale intended to sell his controlling interest to a rival and remove her from the business. Alice explains that she borrowed the brass candlestick for the library telephone table while copying the agreement earlier in the evening. She has a serious motive and a possible route through the main hall. Nothing yet clears her.                                               |
| 9   | **Interview: Bob** — art-authentication comparison                         | Compare the painting’s signature, catalogue entry, restoration marks, and invoice.                                                                                 | The appraisal report from Puzzle 3 concerns Bob: the painting he sold Vale is a forgery. Bob may have been deceived or dishonest; that remains open. He confirms that the marble bust was already in Vale’s bedroom, where it was moved before dinner by the housekeeper. Bob’s route has already been eliminated by the maze, but the interview gives his motive a real cost.                                                      |
| 10  | **Interview: Dave** — family-tree logic puzzle                             | Reconstruct a small family tree from birth records, marriage records, and changed surnames.                                                                        | The altered family record from Puzzle 3 concerns Dave. It shows that Vale could expose a hidden change of identity in Dave’s family. Dave explains that the iron bookend was in the gallery because he used it to hold open a medical journal while searching for his coat. The reader learns Dave’s motive and the object associated with his location, but Dave is not cleared yet.                                               |
| 11  | **Interview: Carl** — newspaper reconstruction and fabric matching         | First restore the torn newspaper clipping from Puzzle 3, then match four fabric samples to the dinner napkins and a fibre caught in the west study door.           | The restored clipping reports the old hit-and-run and prints Vale’s signed claim that Carl was with him at the time. Carl admits Vale recently said he would correct that lie. Separately, the fibre caught in the study door has the herringbone weave of Carl’s folded dinner napkin. This is a quiet physical fact, not an announced solution: it tells the reader that Carl’s napkin passed through the study door.             |
| 12  | **Coffee service** — route-and-observation deduction                       | Reconstruct the butler’s and maid’s coffee-service paths from tray contents, door positions, and short household notes.                                            | The butler’s path covers the main hall between the library and study; Alice would have been seen using it. The maid’s path crosses the gallery doorway; Dave would have been seen entering the study from the gallery. Neither staff member could see the concealed service passage from the drawing room. This puzzle supplies the “could reach the study unseen” facts for the final grid without naming a culprit.               |
| 13  | **Final case file: who killed Vale?** — evidence-synthesis grid            | Fill the case file with each guest’s motive, object fact, route, and whether the route could be taken unseen.                                                      | Carl is the only person with all the necessary elements: a motive to prevent Vale’s confession, the unseen service passage from the drawing room, and a napkin trace that links him to the bronze-horse route. Bob had no route; Alice and Dave had visible routes. After the reader identifies Carl, the detective searches his coat and finds the bronze horse in the torn lining, wrapped in the remainder of the folded napkin. |

## Why the interviews now work

Each interview is a different kind of puzzle and has a self-contained answer:

| Interview | Puzzle type                                   | Immediate answer                                                          | Later value                                                |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Alice     | Financial deduction                           | Vale intended to remove Alice from the business.                          | Motive; candlestick location; route is visible.            |
| Bob       | Visual art comparison                         | The painting is a forgery.                                                | Motive; marble-bust location; no route from conservatory.  |
| Dave      | Family-tree logic                             | Vale had proof of a hidden family identity change.                        | Motive; bookend location; gallery route is visible.        |
| Carl      | Newspaper reconstruction plus fabric matching | Vale gave Carl a false alibi; the study-door fibre matches Carl’s napkin. | Motive; physical link to service-passage route and weapon. |

The reader can enjoy each one as a financial, visual, family, or word-and-pattern puzzle. Its importance only becomes fully clear when the final case-file grid puts all four together.

## Details still open

- The final wording and severity of the old hit-and-run and the other three secrets.
- Whether Bob knowingly sold the forgery.
- The exact nature of Dave’s family identity secret.
- Individual clue wording, difficulty ratings, illustrations, and page count.
