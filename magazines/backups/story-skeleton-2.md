# The Last Dinner at Vale House — revised story skeleton

This draft supersedes the previous skeleton. It gives the case a complete solution but does not create puzzle clues or final artwork. Names remain placeholders.

## Case premise

Walter Vale invites four people who do not know one another to dinner at Vale House. Each receives the same unsettling message: **“At dinner I will settle an unfinished matter between us.”** Vale has prepared four sealed packets of documents for them.

After dinner, Vale leaves the table to collect the packets from his study. The guests disperse through the house while coffee is prepared. The butler enters the study to summon Vale and finds him dead.

The murderer is **Carl, Vale’s nephew**. Years earlier, Vale helped Carl provide a false alibi after Carl caused a hit-and-run collision. Vale has received a telegram from the person who gave that alibi, withdrawing it. Carl kills Vale to prevent the telegram and Vale’s own statement from reaching the police.

Carl reaches the study through a hidden service passage, takes a small bronze horse statuette from the gallery, and strikes Vale with it. He steals the sealed packet addressed to him, wraps the statuette in his folded dinner napkin, and conceals it in the torn lining of his coat.

## The four guests and their reasons to fear Vale

| Guest | Relationship to Vale | What Vale intended to reveal or do |
|---|---|---|
| Alice | Business partner | Vale planned to change their company agreement, sell his controlling interest, and remove Alice from the business she helped build. |
| Bob | Art dealer | Bob sold Vale a painting that has now been shown to be a forgery. It remains undecided whether Bob was deceived himself or knowingly sold it as genuine. |
| Carl | Nephew | Vale planned to give the police a telegram withdrawing Carl’s old false alibi, together with Vale’s own statement. |
| Dave | Doctor | Vale held documents that could expose a concealed change of identity in Dave’s family. The exact history can be decided later. |

All four have a motive. The story does not treat motive alone as proof of murder.

## Puzzle-by-puzzle story

| # | Puzzle and possible form | What the reader does | Exact discovery and story movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The body is discovered** — four-set logic grid | Match each guest to their relationship with Vale, their location when the butler finds the body, and what they say they were doing. | Introduces Alice (business partner), Bob (art dealer), Carl (nephew), and Dave (doctor). Their accounts are: Alice was using the library telephone; Bob was examining the disputed painting in the conservatory; Carl was writing in a notebook in the drawing room; Dave was looking for his coat in the gallery. These are claims, not yet confirmed alibis. |
| 2 | **The dinner table** — visual place-setting match | Match each guest to a seat using overlapping place-setting details: glass garnish, napkin fold, missing spoon or bread, and place position. | Establishes who sat where during dinner. Carl’s place has the only folded napkin. Vale tells the table that he will collect the four sealed packets from his study, then leaves. This lets the reader picture the room and plants the napkin that will later connect Carl to the weapon. |
| 3 | **Reasons to fear Vale** — four-set logic grid | Match each guest to a motive, a document found by the detective, and the place where it was found. | The detective learns the four exact pressures on the guests: Alice’s exclusion from the business, Bob’s forged painting, Carl’s withdrawn alibi, and Dave’s family record. The reader learns that Vale deliberately assembled people whose lives he could damage, but cannot yet tell which grievance turned into murder. |
| 4 | **Vale’s private meetings** — timeline puzzle | Order Vale’s four pre-dinner conversations by cross-referencing the guests’ accounts with household events, such as the arrival of the tea tray, the laying of the table, and the call to dinner. | Carl’s conversation occurs last. Vale tells him that the alibi witness has withdrawn their statement and that the sealed packet contains the telegram and Vale’s confession. Alice, Bob, and Dave each learn about their own problem earlier. The timeline establishes Carl as the guest with the newest and most immediate threat, while keeping all four as plausible suspects. |
| 5 | **The study before and after** — spot-the-difference scene puzzle | Compare the house steward’s pre-dinner inventory drawing with the study and adjoining gallery after the murder. | The reader finds three meaningful changes: the small bronze horse has disappeared from its gallery shelf; the west study door stands open; and Carl’s sealed packet is missing from the group of four. The bronze horse becomes the leading possible weapon, the west door suggests a route, and the missing packet gives the killer a specific purpose. |
| 6 | **The hidden way through Vale House** — literal maze and floor-plan puzzle | Trace every usable route from the guests’ rooms to the study. Doors that are locked, blocked, or one-way are built into the maze. | The conservatory is a dead end during dinner: its inner door is locked from the gallery side and its garden door is bolted. Bob could not reach the study from where he was examining the painting. The drawing room, gallery, and study are linked by a narrow service passage that avoids the main hall. Carl could use it from the drawing room; Dave could use it from the gallery; Alice could still have reached the study by the main corridor. Bob is the first suspect cleared. |
| 7 | **What killed Vale?** — evidence-matching puzzle | Match the wound shape, fragments at the scene, and the candidate objects from the gallery. | The detective’s team confirms that the bronze horse, rather than a knife or a heavy ornament, made the fatal wound. Small bronze flakes are found beside the desk, and the shape of the statue’s raised hoof matches the injury. The missing object is now the murder weapon, not merely an item that was moved. |
| 8 | **Interview: Alice** — document comparison puzzle | Compare the original business agreement, Vale’s planned amendment, and Alice’s annotations. | Alice admits that Vale intended to force her out of the business by selling his controlling interest to a rival. She also admits she entered the study earlier that evening to copy part of the amendment. This proves access and gives her a stronger motive, but it does not place her in the study after dinner. Alice remains a viable suspect. |
| 9 | **Interview: Bob** — painting-authentication puzzle | Compare the painting’s signature, catalogue entry, restoration marks, and invoice. | The painting is a forgery. The reader learns why Bob feared Vale, and that Bob may have known more than he first admitted. However, the maze has already shown that Bob could not get from the conservatory to the study during the relevant interval. His secret remains consequential for the story, but he is not the murderer. |
| 10 | **Interview: Dave** — call-record and statement puzzle | Reconstruct Dave’s telephone consultation from the answering-service record, the patient’s message, and the gallery telephone log. | Dave was on a sustained medical call from the gallery while Vale was away from the table and until the body was discovered. The call is supported by an outside caller and the answering service. Dave’s family secret remains real, but he could not have left the gallery, taken the horse, and killed Vale. Dave is the only additional suspect cleared through an interview. Alice and Carl remain. |
| 11 | **Interview: Carl** — paper-trace comparison puzzle | Compare Carl’s notebook, the seal of the packet addressed to him, the folded napkin, and traces at the study’s west door. | Carl’s claimed notebook entry cannot be the account he says he wrote after dinner: it was copied from an earlier note already in the drawing room. A torn fragment of his folded napkin is caught in the west study door; its herringbone weave matches the napkin from Carl’s place. The fold also contains a broken piece of the stag-shaped seal from Carl’s packet. Carl had the packet and used the napkin in the service route, but the detective still presents the final conclusion as a deduction rather than an immediate accusation. |
| 12 | **Final case file: who killed Vale?** — evidence-synthesis deduction | Use the recorded facts to assign the route, weapon, missing packet, and physical traces to one person. | Carl is the only person who fits every fact. Bob lacked a route to the study; Dave was on the call; Alice had motive and access but no link to the weapon, packet, or service passage. Carl had the urgent reason to stop Vale, a direct route from the drawing room, the missing packet’s broken seal, and the napkin trace at the study door. After the reader identifies him, the detective searches Carl’s coat and finds the bronze horse in the torn lining, wrapped in the remainder of the folded napkin. |

## Case-file facts for the finale

The final puzzle can ask readers to record these facts as they work through the book:

1. Vale left dinner to retrieve four sealed packets from his study.
2. The bronze horse disappeared from the gallery and caused the fatal wound.
3. Carl’s packet was the only packet missing after the murder.
4. Bob could not leave the conservatory to reach the study.
5. Dave was on a confirmed medical call in the gallery.
6. Alice had a real motive and access, but no evidence linking her to the horse, packet, or service passage.
7. Carl’s folded napkin, packet seal, notebook, and coat lining connect him to the route and weapon.
